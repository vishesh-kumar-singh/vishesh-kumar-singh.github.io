One of the best and most famous lines from *One Piece* has always been: "When does a man die? When he is hit by a bullet? No! When he suffers from a disease? No! When he ate a soup made out of a poisonous mushroom? No! A man dies when he is forgotten."

That sentence has always had two faces: one with which you can keep a dead man alive, and another with which you can kill someone without them ever dying. Everyone thinks of the first one, but no one thinks of what happens if you are forgotten while you are still alive. Would you call yourself murdered?

Maybe it really does feel like being killed when you are forgotten by someone you can never forget. When just the thought of someone makes your chest as heavy as being crushed by an elephant, when just a random name you hear in public makes your soul shiver, every single time... maybe it really does feel like being shot by a bullet directly through the heart.

An even bigger question would be: what happens if you are asked to forget someone you never wanted dead? Does it feel like murder? It is the most brutal form of murder known to humankind, I would say. And the person dying is always you. Maybe that is why it feels easier to keep thinking about her than to forget her. The thought of her hurts like hell, but the opposite? The thought of not even being able to think about her?

Maybe it is a lot easier to keep thinking and be murdered daily by someone else than to forget and kill yourself.

---

So it is her birthday today. She must have enjoyed the celebration.

But how did my day start? What was the first thing I saw before my eyes? A screenshot of an Instagram story, sent by a dear friend. The story had five pictures of hers. I already had the top-right one, it was an old picture; the others were new. The most special was the picture in the center. It was her, in a black kurti... looking even more gorgeous than ever.

Her hands? They were holding someone else's hand, just like she held mine that day. "Pagli ne maarne ke baad jala bhi diya... antim sanskar bhi ho gaya." (She even burned me after killing... the final rites are complete now as well.)

But what about her smile in that image? I just can't unsee that wonderful smile in that image and in the other one in which she was holding a flower bouquet.

> "For that smile, I wish I could bring down the world to her feet, but I can't... because that world of mine is holding onto someone else too tightly."

---

The ability to grieve over something is the worst thing that humans possess.

When do you grieve? Over someone close who has died? Yeah, we do. Just think about someone very special, and now you know you will never be able to see them because they are dead, they aren't here anymore. But what if they are here, living happily, and you still know you will never be able to see them?

The second one is definitely heavier, and the reason for that is a mechanism that has sustained humankind for centuries: hope. You know a dead person will never meet you, but when it comes to the living, our hopes and our imaginations lie to us. A line I once heard went:

> Tumne jo khat kabhi likha nahi, uske jawab mai roz likhta hu. (I write the answers to the letters you never wrote.)

And this line will hit anyone who has run millions of mental simulations of that person meeting them just once. Hope is what has helped humans stay strong in the toughest situations. But when you know that hope is a lie, yet you still choose to live in that world of imagination rather than reality, maybe that means you are either a fool or at that second-to-last stage of love known as *Junoon* (madness).

Now when you look at the story of Laila Majnu again, just think for a second: did Majnu see Laila everywhere because he lost his mind from trauma and couldn't tell reality from imagination, or did he always know it was just his imagination, but found it far more comforting to stay there than face reality?

---

The most ironic thing right now is that I am on the same train we once took together. Traveling from Varanasi to Kanpur, this time alone. And it has just pulled into Prayagraj as I type this at 16:59, the city where she is.

I'm wearing her favorite t-shirt, like I did while while coming to kanpur just in hope of what if... she boards the same train?? And all I can do right now is keep staring out in the direction of her college, wondering what she must be doing at this moment. How happy she must be today on her birthday, how her friends must be wishing her, what dress she is wearing, how she is celebrating, and what not. Is she with him right now?

I have probably imagined hundreds of different scenarios of what she might be doing right now. But why? Why am I doing this? Why am I writing this, even when it brings tears to my eyes?

---

People ask: if she is happy in her life and never thinks about you, why do you have to keep thinking about her? Why waste your present over someone who doesn't care about you anymore?

And I ask myself the exact same thing...
